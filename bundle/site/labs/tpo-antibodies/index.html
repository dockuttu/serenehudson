TPO Antibodies (Thyroid Peroxidase) Test: What It Measures and Why It Matters

TPO antibodies are made when the immune system mistakenly targets the thyroid. They’re the key marker for autoimmune thyroid disease.

What the TPO antibodies test measures

  • Antibodies against thyroid peroxidase, an enzyme your thyroid uses to make hormone

Why we check it at Serene

High TPO antibodies are a sign of Hashimoto’s disease, the most common cause of an underactive thyroid, and they can show up before thyroid hormone levels change. Knowing this early helps your physician watch your thyroid more closely.

At Serene Med Spa in Hudson, Ohio, every hormone and wellness plan starts with Labcorp lab work reviewed by a board-certified physician.

What your results can mean

Higher than normal

High TPO antibodies suggest autoimmune thyroid disease, most often Hashimoto’s. The higher the level, the more likely an autoimmune thyroid condition.

Lower than normal

A negative or low result makes autoimmune thyroid disease less likely, though it doesn’t rule out every thyroid problem.

Normal ranges vary by lab, age and sex. Your physician reads every result together with your symptoms, history and other tests.

How to prepare

No fasting is needed. If you take biotin (vitamin B7) or a hair, skin and nail supplement, tell us first. The FDA warns that high-dose biotin can interfere with some lab tests, including hormone and thyroid tests.
